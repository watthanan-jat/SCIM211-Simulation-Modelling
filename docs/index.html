SCIM211

Simulation Modelling

Instructor: Asst. Prof. Dr. Watthanan Jatuviriyapornchai

Simulation modelling is used when real systems are too complex, expensive, risky, or time-consuming to study directly. This course introduces how to represent a system, describe uncertainty, generate random behaviour, run computational experiments, and interpret simulation output.

Weekly theory sessions introduce the modelling and statistical concepts, while the lab sessions develop a continuing coffee-shop simulation that grows from a simple queue into a tool for comparison, validation, and decision-making.

What You Will Learn

Course Description

The importance of simulation; example models; generating random numbers; simulating random variables; Monte Carlo simulation; modelling inputs; analysing outputs; Markov chains and discrete events; discrete-event simulation; variance reduction techniques; model validation; and computer techniques for simulation.

What This Course Builds

Course Learning Outcomes

CLO1

Explain the fundamental concepts and importance of simulation modelling in solving complex problems.

CLO2

Generate random numbers and random variates from specified probability distributions using standard computational techniques.

CLO3

Implement Monte Carlo simulation methods and discrete-event models to solve problems in various application domains.

CLO4

Analyse simulation inputs and outputs using appropriate statistical techniques to create accurate model representations and draw valid conclusions.

CLO5

Construct Markov chain models to represent stochastic processes in real-world systems.

CLO6

Evaluate simulation models through systematic validation approaches and appropriate tools for different problem domains.

What You Will Build

Lab Pathway

All seven labs form one semester-long Coffee Shop Simulation Project, not seven separate exercises. Starting with a busy cafe, you will gradually model customer arrivals, drink choices, service times, baristas, queues, profit, customer loyalty, and uncertainty.

Each lab unlocks one new part of the same model. You will build Python simulations step by step, connect probability to practical decisions, use plots, tables, and confidence intervals to explain results, and develop clear assumptions, limitations, and recommendations. Everything carries forward to the Week 16 Project Challenge.

Lab 1 · Map the Café

Enter the System

Investigate the customers, baristas, resources, events, and queues, then run your first coffee-shop simulation by hand and in Python.
Lab 2 · Week 4

Crack the Randomness Code

Discover how computers create repeatable “random” numbers and use them to generate the cafe’s unpredictable arrivals.

Lab 3 · Week 6

Bring the Café to Life

Turn random numbers into realistic arrival and drink-service times so every simulated customer has a different experience.

Lab 4 · Week 8

Should We Run the Promotion?

Use Monte Carlo experiments to compare profit and risk, then decide whether a new promotion is worth launching.

Lab 5 · Week 10

Build the Queue Engine

Make the simulation clock tick as customers arrive, wait, order, and leave, and reveal where the longest delays occur.

Lab 6 · Week 12

Predict Who Comes Back

Model customer loyalty with Markov chains and explore how today’s experience may change tomorrow’s behaviour.

Lab 7 · Week 14

Can You Trust Your Model?

Challenge your assumptions, compare the model with evidence, and turn simulation output into a recommendation you can defend.

Final Mission · Week 16

Face the Project Challenge

Bring every piece together as a team, adapt your simulation to an assigned scenario, and defend the decision your evidence supports.

Mini Project

Coffee Shop Simulation Decision Challenge

Work in a group to build a reusable coffee-shop simulation model and compare a baseline policy with an improvement policy. In Week 16, your group will apply the model it has built to an assigned Project Challenge.

Build

Model arrivals, drink choices, service times, baristas, queues, outputs, and optional loyalty behaviour.

Compare

Evaluate a baseline system against a policy such as adding a barista, simplifying the menu, or running a promotion.

Defend

Use replications, confidence intervals, validation checks, and limitations to support a practical recommendation.
