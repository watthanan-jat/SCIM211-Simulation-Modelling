# SCIM211 Assessment

Assessment in SCIM211 Simulation Modelling measures conceptual understanding, computational skill, statistical reasoning, model construction, validation, and communication.

## Assessment Components

| Assessment | Weight | Key information |
|---|---:|---|
| Midterm examination | 25% | Individual written examination |
| Final examination | 25% | Individual written examination |
| Lab exercises | 21% | Seven assessed hands-on lab sessions |
| Quizzes | 20% | Four quizzes offered; best two scores count |
| Group Mini Project | 9% | Group simulation project and Week 16 Project Challenge |
| **Total** | **100%** |  |

Students may be asked to explain their code, calculations, modelling choices, and conclusions orally to demonstrate individual understanding.

## Examinations

The examination component is worth 50% in total:

- Midterm examination: 25%
- Final examination: 25%

There is no re-examination offered as part of this course. Faculty and University regulations apply to examinations and requests based on exceptional circumstances.

## Lab Exercises

Seven lab exercises contribute 21% of the final grade.

### Attendance and Completion

- Attendance is compulsory and will be monitored.
- If you cannot attend a lab session, inform the instructor in advance.
- Each lab contains several required exercises. Complete all exercises to be eligible for full marks.
- A missed lab requires a valid reason. Medical absence requires supporting medical documentation.
- Students remain responsible for making up missed learning activities in their own time.

### Understanding and Assistance

- During assessment, you may be asked questions to demonstrate that you understand the problem, your code, and your solution.
- Marks may be withheld when a student cannot explain submitted work.
- The instructor and lab assistants will help students think through problems, but they will not write or debug programs for students.

### Strict AI Rule for Labs

AI tools may be used as learning aids during lab sessions. Students remain fully responsible for checking, understanding, and explaining all AI-assisted work.

Copying or submitting AI-generated code, answers, or explanations without understanding them is irresponsible AI use.

> **A student who cannot demonstrate an understanding of AI-assisted work will receive a score of 0 for that lab.**

Students must also follow the full course [AI Policy](ai_policy.md).

### Lab Scoring Guide

| Score | Description |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Absent, completes less than 50% of the exercises, or cannot demonstrate understanding of AI-assisted work |
| 1 | Completes at least 50% of the exercises |
| 2 | Completes all exercises |
| 3 | Completes all exercises and answers questions promptly |

### Lab Submission Deadline

- Submit each lab during its scheduled session whenever possible.
- Work for Lab *n* may also be submitted before noon (12:00) at the following lab session, Lab *n+1*.
- No marks will be awarded after that deadline.
- Submission instructions and submission links will be provided through Google Classroom.

## Group Mini Project and Project Challenge

The Mini Project is worth 9% and is completed in groups.

Throughout the semester, each group will build a reusable coffee-shop simulation model. The model should support changes to inputs such as arrival rates, service times, demand, staffing, and operating policies without requiring the entire program to be rewritten.

In Week 16, each group will receive an assigned **Project Challenge**. The group must:

1. apply the simulation model it has built;
2. make the required changes for the assigned scenario;
3. run appropriate experiments and replications;
4. interpret the results and uncertainty;
5. support a practical recommendation with evidence; and
6. explain important assumptions and limitations.

The detailed project outline will be released later in the course.

## Plagiarism Policy

Plagiarism of any kind—whether from peers, published sources, past work, or AI—**violates academic integrity**.

### Plagiarism Includes

- Copying text, code, charts, data, or ideas **without citation**.
- Submitting another person’s work **as your own**.
- Reusing your own past submissions **without permission**.
- Paraphrasing from a source **without acknowledgment**.
- Presenting AI-generated material as entirely your own work.

### Penalties

- **First offence:** A score of **0** for the affected assessment and a formal warning.
- **Further or serious offences:** Escalation to the academic committee and possible failure of the course, subject to Faculty and University regulations.

### How to Avoid Plagiarism

- Do your own work unless collaboration is explicitly permitted.
- When using references, code, data, or external help, **cite and reflect on them properly**.
- Distinguish clearly between your contribution and material obtained from others.
- Ask the instructor if you are unsure whether a form of assistance or reuse is allowed.

## AI Usage in This Course

This course follows **AI Collaboration — Level 3**, as described in MUIC’s *AI in Teaching and Learning: A Practical Guide to Implementing the AI Assessment Scale*.

Students are encouraged to use AI tools as collaborators to support learning, particularly for drafting, idea generation, and feedback. However, all submitted work must demonstrate **critical engagement, meaningful human revision, and independent thinking**.

### Permitted Uses

AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini may be used:

- to generate ideas, brainstorm, or clarify course concepts;
- to help structure or refine written reports and presentations;
- to practise simulation scenarios;
- to request feedback on wording or explanations; and
- to receive suggestions about code after making an honest attempt.

### Not Permitted

Students must not:

- submit AI-generated answers directly as Lab Exercises;
- submit AI-generated project reports or models without meaningful human revision;
- use AI without personal interpretation or understanding;
- submit code, calculations, modelling decisions, or conclusions that they cannot explain;
- fabricate data, results, references, or evidence using AI; or
- use AI-generated content without acknowledgment or critical modification.

> **If a Lab Exercise relies solely on AI output and the student cannot demonstrate personal understanding, that lab will receive a score of 0.**

### Required AI Use Acknowledgment Statement

When AI tools have been used for the group project, students must include an **AI Use Acknowledgment Statement** as an appendix.

The statement must identify:

- **which AI tools were used**, such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini;
- **how each tool was used**, such as idea generation, grammar correction, code feedback, or report structuring; and
- a **brief reflection** explaining how the AI output was evaluated, corrected, revised, or integrated into the group’s work.

Failure to include this statement when AI tools were used may result in a grade penalty or academic review.

### Academic Integrity Reminder

Students are responsible for ensuring that every submission reflects their own understanding rather than the uncritical output of an AI tool. AI misuse will be treated as a violation of academic integrity.

**Reference:** [MUIC — AI in Teaching and Learning: A Practical Guide to Implementing the AI Assessment Scale](https://muic.mahidol.ac.th/th/news/ai-in-teaching-and-learning-a-practical-guide-to-implementing-the-ai-assessment-scale/)

## Feedback

Feedback will focus on:

- mathematical and computational correctness;
- modelling judgement and assumptions;
- code clarity and reproducibility;
- experimental design;
- interpretation of uncertainty;
- validation and limitations; and
- written and oral communication.

Students should use feedback from earlier labs to improve later labs and the group Mini Project.
