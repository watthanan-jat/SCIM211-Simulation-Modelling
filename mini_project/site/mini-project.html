Café Poisson: Coffee Shop Simulation Hackathon

Café Poisson has just opened next to campus, and the owner needs advice they can trust. In a group of three, build a simulation of the café. On hackathon day the owner sends you one urgent question: you have one hour to adapt your model, and four minutes to pitch your answer.

Test your code Download Python template

Your Mission

Build the café in Python. Then answer the owner's question.

The owner named the café after the Poisson process, because the customers really do arrive at random. Every week brings a new question: another barista for lunch? A matcha promotion? What if the coffee machine slows down? Ideas like these cannot be tested on real customers, so the owner has hired your group.

The café is open all day, but lunch is the rush, and your model covers the lunch session (12:00–14:00); at 14:00 the door closes for a one-hour staff break. During lunch, two baristas serve about 30 customers an hour from one line. Classic drinks (Americano, latte, Thai tea…) take about 2 minutes, sell for ฿85 and use ฿25 of ingredients; specialty drinks (matcha latte, frappé, smoothie…) take about 4 minutes, sell for ฿120 and use ฿40 of ingredients. Each barista costs ฿120 an hour for the full two hours. Customers give up after 5–15 minutes of waiting, and only 8 people fit in the line.

You do not need the most complicated café. You need a model that is correct, easy to change, reproducible (the same seed gives the same result), and useful for a decision. Every member of the group must understand the code and be ready to explain it.

Minimum Model

What your café model must have

01

Lunch session

A 120-minute lunch session (12:00–14:00), with accepted customers allowed to finish after the door closes.

02

Random demand

Random arrivals ordering classic or specialty drinks.

03

Random service

Service times that depend on order type and use the supplied seed.

04

Resources

A configurable number of identical baristas.

05

Queue

One FCFS queue with a configurable maximum length; customers who find it full balk and are lost.

06

Impatient customers

Customers whose patience runs out before service starts leave the queue (renege) and are lost.

07

Event logic

Arrival, service-start, service-completion, and patience-expiry events on one simulation clock.

08

Economics and outputs

Customer counts, waiting measures, utilisation, revenue, ingredient and staffing cost, and profit.

09

Replication

One result per supplied seed so uncertainty can be estimated.

10

Flexibility

Inputs must come from config; do not hard-code the baseline values.

Model rules every group must follow
PartRequired rule
ArrivalsPoisson process: independent exponential gaps with mean 60 / arrival_rate_per_hour minutes, during [0, run_minutes). A rate of 0 means nobody arrives.
Order typeClassic (simple) with probability simple_order_probability, otherwise specialty (complex), independently for each customer.
Service timeExponential with mean mean_service_simple (classic) or mean_service_complex (specialty).
PatienceUniform between 0.5 * mean_patience and 1.5 * mean_patience. A customer who has not started service by arrival time + patience reneges and is lost. None means customers never renege.
Queue limitmax_queue counts customers waiting, not those in service. An arrival who finds every barista busy and max_queue customers waiting balks and is lost. None means no limit.
DisciplineOne FCFS queue; a free barista serves the longest-waiting customer still in the queue.
End of sessionAt run_minutes the door closes for the staff break. Accepted customers finish; customers still waiting may renege after the door closes.

What the checker tests

Contract checks

  • The file loads and defines both required functions.
  • The official configuration works without editing the file, and the configuration dictionary is not changed.
  • Required results are numeric, finite, nonnegative where appropriate, and internally consistent.
  • Customer counts, order counts, revenue, cost, profit, and throughput balance.
  • No served customer waited longer than the largest possible patience.
  • The same seed reproduces a run and a different seed changes it.
  • Zero demand, single-drink menus, max_queue = 0, and None settings behave correctly.
  • The replication function returns one correct row per seed.

Statistical checks

Your model is run for 100 seeds in each of six configurations (standard café, single barista, busy café with a short queue limit, very impatient customers, larger café with a different menu and prices, long quiet day). The average of each output is compared with the reference model. Your numbers will not match exactly, and they do not need to: the check fails only when the difference is more than 5 standard errors, which a correct model almost never produces. These checks run your model about 600 times and usually take under a minute.

Week 16 Wednesday Lab

Hackathon day: the owner's question

Hour 1 · No AI, offline

Answer the owner's question

  1. Adapt a copy of the prepared simulation; keep cafe_model.py unchanged.
  2. Compare the required policies.
  3. Use the seeds printed on your card.
  4. Run at least 40 replications per policy.
  5. Prepare one table, one graph, and a 95% confidence interval.
  6. Make a recommendation and identify one limitation.
  7. After your pitch, hand in your changed code, the results table, the graph, and your pitch slides (the same files you used in your pitch), in the way announced at the start of the session.
Hour 2

Pitch to the owner

Each group receives six minutes: four minutes to pitch and two minutes for questions. All three members must speak.

  1. Student 1: model and assigned problem.
  2. Student 2: experiment and results.
  3. Student 3: recommendation and limitation.

Your pitch slides. Use as many slides as you like, but keep to four minutes. Prepare the first part at home: a title slide, and your café model (how a customer moves through the café, the main rules, and your checks). On the day, add the challenge at the end: the owner's question in one sentence; what you changed in the model; the plans you compared (same seeds, at least 40 runs); your table and graph, with the main difference and its 95% confidence interval (if it includes 0, say the plans cannot be told apart); one sentence of advice the owner can act on; and one limitation that could change it. Remember that the owner runs a café, not a statistics class: give your numbers in baht, minutes and customers.

AI tools, the internet, phones, and other personal devices are not allowed, do not talk to other groups, and using AI or the internet is an academic-integrity violation.
