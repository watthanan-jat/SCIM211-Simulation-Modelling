What should I observe?

Variation shrinks, but not in a straight line

The estimate usually settles nearer to pi as N grows, but it can move away temporarily. Convergence is a long-run tendency, not a guarantee of improvement at every step.

Student challenge

  1. Record the estimate at N = 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000.
  2. Observe whether the estimate improves monotonically.
  3. Explain why different runs produce different estimates.
  4. Classify the model as static or dynamic and deterministic or stochastic.
Model assumptions
  • Each point is sampled independently and uniformly from the square.
  • The circle has radius 1 and the square covers [-1, 1] by [-1, 1].
  • A point on the circle boundary is counted as inside.
  • The pseudo-random number generator and seed determine the sample path.

What should I observe?

Local contact creates varied epidemic paths

Infection can disappear quickly, spread around the ring, or stall behind recovered particles. The same inputs can produce different peaks and durations because every transition is probabilistic.

Student challenge

  1. Run the same parameter values three times.
  2. Compare the sample paths, peak infection, and epidemic duration.
  3. Reduce p while holding q fixed.
  4. Increase q while holding p fixed.
  5. Explain why identical parameters can produce different outcomes.
  6. Classify the model as dynamic or static, deterministic or stochastic, and discrete or continuous.
Model assumptions
  • The population is closed and arranged on a ring.
  • Each particle interacts only with its two nearest neighbours.
  • All state changes are calculated from time t and applied together at time t + 1.
  • If a susceptible particle has k infected neighbours, its infection probability is 1 - (1 - p)k.
  • An infected particle recovers with probability q; recovery is permanent.
